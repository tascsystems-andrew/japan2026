The last one out

Andrew is in Canada and Dan and Connie flew home on the 23rd, so this is Sam, Adrienne and the four children. Vintage shopping in Amerikamura, the covered arcades of Shinsaibashi-suji when small legs need a roof, and Dotonbori after dark — which the family did on 7 November, but not at night and not with Adrienne.

It is a Saturday, and Adrienne flies at 14:00 tomorrow. Amerikamura on a Saturday is heaving, and Shinsaibashi-suji is the covered escape when it stops being fun. Do not make it a late one.
10:00

Amerikamura

Chuo Ward · Free · Shops open ~11:00

Osaka's answer to Harajuku — a compact neighbourhood of vintage clothing stores, independent boutiques, record shops, street art, and coffee shops built around a small triangle park. It's the youth culture hub of the city, grittier and more fun than Tokyo's version.

The vibe is thrift stores next to skate shops next to tiny cafés. For Adrienne coming from four days in Tokyo, this is a completely different energy — less polished, more personality. The Big Step shopping centre anchors the area and has a good mix of local brands.

🧒 Kids love: The colourful murals and street art, the gachapon machines on every corner, and the general energy of a neighbourhood full of interesting-looking people and interesting-looking things.

Tip: The vintage shops are small and packed — strollers might need to stay outside. Triangle Park is a good kid-corralling spot while adults browse nearby shops.

Minami · nothing booked

Nothing booked and nothing planned. Amerikamura and Shinsaibashi are thick with places to eat and you will be walking past twenty of them.

Tip: Tako no Tetsu — the make-your-own takoyaki one — is not today. That moved to the Umeda day on the 20th, so do not go looking for it here.

🚃 Amerikamura to Shinsaibashi-suji — two minutes on foot
14:00

Shinsaibashi-suji

Chuo Ward · Free · Covered, end to end

The covered shopping arcade that runs north from Namba — six hundred metres of roof, which is the entire point when you are out with four children on a Saturday in late November. Amerikamura is two minutes west of it, so you can duck between the two as the weather and the mood require.

Everything from drugstores to department stores. It is also the sensible place to spend the last of anyone’s shopping list, since Adrienne flies tomorrow.

🧒 Kids love: the gachapon machines, the crane games, and being under a roof.

17:30

Dotonbori

Chuo Ward · Free · Best after dark

The beating heart of Osaka — and Adrienne's first time seeing it. A canal lined with massive neon signs, the running Glico Man, the mechanical crab, packed with people and the best street food in Japan. This is Osaka at full volume, and it hits completely different when you're seeing it for the first time.

Even for D&C on their second visit, Dotonbori after dark is a different animal. Walk the canal strip, cross the bridges, eat everything — gyoza, yakitori, more takoyaki (you're experts now), okonomiyaki. Finish at Hozenji Yokocho, the tiny lantern-lit alley with the moss-covered shrine, for a quiet contrast to the neon chaos.

🧒 Kids love: The sensory overload — neon everywhere, the Glico Man, street food they can pick and choose, and showing Adrienne everything they already know about Osaka like little tour guides.

Tip: Walk to Ebisubashi Bridge for the classic Glico Man group photo. This is the one everyone needs.

🍢 Why the Takoyaki Lunch Matters

This isn't just a meal — it's the group activity for the trip. Everyone's been in Japan for over two weeks by now, the kids are comfortable, D&C are in their groove, and Adrienne just arrived fresh from Tokyo. Making takoyaki together at the table is the kind of chaotic, messy, funny shared experience that bonds a group. The kids will be terrible at flipping them. The adults won't be much better. Everyone eats their own creations. It's perfect.
