Suggested Route

A Full Day in Kyoto

You stayed overnight at the Oriental Hotel Kyoto Rokujo while the family headed back to Osaka. Today is all yours — no schedule, no kids, no obligations. Here's a suggested route through some of Kyoto's best hits, but feel free to shuffle, swap, or throw it all out and wander. It's your day.

The one fixed point is the end of it: you're riding the Aoniyoshi home — a sightseeing limited express that takes the scenic route back to Osaka via Nara. It leaves Kyoto at 16:50 and it won't wait, so the afternoon is built backwards from that.

Getting around: Your Suica cards work on Kyoto's buses and trains. Google Maps is your best friend for routes — just type the destination name in English and it'll give you the right bus number. Taxis are also easy to flag down if you'd rather not figure out the buses.
Luggage: Check out in the morning and ask the hotel to hold your bags — most Japanese hotels will store luggage for free even after checkout. The hotel is a few minutes from Kyoto Station, so collect them on your way back through at around 16:15. Don't leave it later than that; the Aoniyoshi boards at 16:40.
8:00

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Ukyo Ward · Free · Open 24 hours

If you're up for an early start, the bamboo grove is magical in the morning before the crowds arrive. The towering stalks filter the light in a way that photographs can't capture. The main path loops through the grove and down to the Togetsukyo Bridge for river views.

Tip: If you go, aim for early — by 10am the paths are shoulder-to-shoulder. 8am is golden hour.

🚌 Bus #28 or JR Sagano Line → Kyoto Station area (~40 min). Then Bus #101 or #205 to Kinkaku-ji (~30 min).
10:00

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

Kita Ward · ¥500 · 9:00–17:00

The most photographed building in Kyoto — a three-story golden pavilion reflected in a mirror-still pond. It's touristy for a reason. The grounds are beautiful even beyond the main view, with a moss garden and tea house where you can grab matcha and a sweet.

Tip: Your admission "ticket" is actually a paper charm (ofuda) — keep it as a souvenir.

🚌 Bus #12 or #205 to Shijo-Kawaramachi area (~30 min)
12:00

Nishiki Market

Nakagyo Ward · Free to browse · ~10:00–17:00 (varies by stall)

Kyoto's 400-year-old covered market — five blocks of food stalls, pickles, fresh seafood, knives, sweets, and samples. Great for a graze-lunch — skip the sit-down restaurant and just wander from stall to stall. The tamagoyaki (sweet rolled omelette) and fresh mochi are highlights.

Tip: The market runs one block north of Shijo-dori, between Teramachi and Takakura. Look for the arched entrance.

🚶 Walk east across the river to Gion-Shijo Station (~10 min) → Keihan Line local south to Fushimi-Inari Station (~7 min).
13:30

Fushimi Inari

Fushimi Ward · Free · Open 24 hours

The famous 10,000 vermilion torii gates, climbing the hillside in an unbroken tunnel. You don't need to hike the full mountain — the first fifteen minutes up the trail gives you the iconic tunnel-of-gates photo, and the crowds thin noticeably once you're past the initial stretch.

Tip: Give it about an hour. The station sits directly across the road from the main gate, so there's no walk at either end — this is why it's worth doing before the sake museum rather than after.

🚃 Keihan Line local south, 4 stops to Chushojima Station (~8 min), then a short walk to the brewery district.
14:50

Gekkeikan Ōkura Sake Museum

Fushimi Ward · ¥600 (includes 3 sake tastings) · 9:30–16:30

If you enjoy sake (or are curious about it), Fushimi is one of Japan's top three brewing regions, and Gekkeikan has been making sake here since 1637. The museum is housed in a renovated 1909 brewery — traditional cedar fermentation tanks, centuries-old brewing tools, and the story of how this neighbourhood became synonymous with sake.

Admission is ¥600 and includes 3 tasting tokens — choose from 8 different sakes plus a plum wine option. Extra tokens are just ¥100 each. If you visit in November, look for the fresh green sugidama (cedar ball) hanging at the brewery entrance — it signals the year's new sake has just been brewed.

Tip: You've got about an hour here, which is enough for the museum and the tastings but not for lingering. Leave by 15:45 to make the train comfortably.

🚃 Keihan to Tofukuji (~8 min) → transfer to the JR Nara Line for Kyoto Station (~3 min). Or taxi (~20 min, ~¥2,500). Collect your bags from the hotel on the way through.
16:50

The Aoniyoshi Home

Kintetsu Kyoto Station · Reserved seats, booked for you

Rather than the ordinary commuter train home, you're riding the Aoniyoshi — Kintetsu's sightseeing limited express, and an event in its own right. Four carriages in deep imperial purple with gold Tempyo-era patterning, styled after the temples of Nara: twin seats facing each other across a side table, salon booths for four, and a boutique car with a wooden counter selling snacks and drinks.

It takes the scenic way home — Kyoto down to Nara, then west across to Osaka, rather than the direct line. About 85 minutes, most of it through the countryside south of Kyoto, arriving Osaka-Namba at 18:16.

Tip: Every seat is reserved and it sells out in autumn, so Andrew is booking this well ahead. All you need to do is be on the platform by 16:40. Kintetsu's platforms are inside Kyoto Station, on the south side.

🚶 Step off at Osaka-Namba and you're already there — Dotonbori is a five-minute walk up from the platform.
18:30

Dinner in Dotonbori

Your choice

The train lands you in the best possible spot to finish the day. Dotonbori is Osaka at full volume — the neon canal, the Glico running man, takoyaki stands and okonomiyaki counters and more kushikatsu than anyone needs. After a quiet day of temples and sake, it's a good contrast.

Tip: If you'd rather something calmer after the train, the streets just east of the canal around Hozenji Yokocho are lantern-lit, stone-paved and much quieter — a minute's walk from the noise.

Getting home after dinner: You're already in Osaka. From Osaka-Namba it's four stops on the Hanshin Namba Line straight to Nishikujo — the same station you arrived into, no transfer, about eight minutes. Covered by your Suica.
