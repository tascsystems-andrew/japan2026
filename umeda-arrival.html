Arrival Day

Straight Off the Plane and Into Umeda

You've been on a plane overnight and your body thinks it's the middle of yesterday — as far as your internal clock is concerned it's still the evening of November 5. The plan is deliberately gentle: land late morning, reset at the house, then take the stroller test and spend the evening in Umeda. Shops and dinner are flexible, but the aim is to stay out until at least 20:00 Japan time before heading home.

If browsing feels like too much, settle into an easy Umeda dinner, dessert or an indoor wander. Ordinary flight tiredness changes the activity, not the 20:00 target; a child who is genuinely overwhelmed is the exception.

The airport and the 20:00 stay-out target anchor the day. KE723 lands at 10:45 and the driver tracks the flight, waiting free for 90 minutes from touchdown — so we want to be out of the arrivals hall by about 12:15. After the car, shift the shops and dinner as needed, but treat 20:00 as the earliest ordinary departure from Umeda, not bedtime.
One card to sort, and it's the first job of the evening. Everyone else already has a Suica from the February trip, and those work across Kansai unchanged — but Danae has just turned six, which moves her into the child fare bracket, so she needs a card of her own. We lean on these constantly, so handle it at Nishi-Kujo before boarding the Loop Line. The station has an operator-assisted Midori-no-Kenbaiki Plus machine; there is no staffed Midori-no-Madoguchi counter there.

Korean Air Lounge · ICN T2

Connection reset at Incheon · Everyone

Clear the connection path, regroup in the lounge and use the long layover for breakfast, coffee and a proper reset before the 08:50 flight to Osaka.

Leave the lounge with enough time to reach the gate together; the next flight is the one that lands us in Japan.

10:45

Land at Kansai Airport

KIX · Immigration, bags, customs

KIX is built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, so the approach is all water right up until the wheels touch. Immigration and customs usually run about an hour — Japan's entry process is orderly and unhurried, and it goes faster if you fill in the arrival card on the plane rather than at the desk.

Our driver is already tracking the flight and will wait free for 90 minutes from landing, so the target is to be through and outside by about 12:15. With eight of us and a pile of bags that means heading for the exit rather than browsing. If we do run over, the surcharge is ¥2,000 per half hour paid to the driver in cash — not a disaster, just worth avoiding.

Meeting point is Terminal 1, 1F — exit C if we come out of the north exit, exit D if it's the south. The driver waits outside the doors with a name board.

Tip: There are free water fountains past customs, and the airport toilets are worth experiencing on their own terms. Consider it your first cultural encounter.

🚗 Straight out to the car — the driver meets us at the 1F exit with a name board.
13:15

The House in Konohana

Kasugadekita · allow about 25–35 min on foot to JR Nishi-Kujo

The drive out crosses the Sky Gate Bridge — the span that ties the airport island to the mainland — then runs up along the bay before the city closes in around you. An undemanding first look at Osaka through a car window.

Our Airbnb for the six weeks sits in a quiet residential pocket of Konohana Ward. It's a real neighbourhood rather than a tourist strip — vending machines on the corners, bicycles everywhere, a convenience store at the end of the road. JR Nishi-Kujo is roughly 1.7 km away in a straight line; allow about 25–35 minutes on foot with two strollers and check live walking directions before leaving.

You've got a couple of unhurried hours here — unpack properly, shower, work out which switch does what. If everyone is up for it, aim to start the stroller walk a little after 16:00 to reach Nishi-Kujo before the child-card errand.

Tip: A shower and a change of clothes does more for jet lag than a nap does. The nap is a trap — at this hour it's the difference between sleeping tonight and being wide awake at 3am.

~16:05

Walk both strollers to JR Nishi-Kujo

First stroller test · allow 25–35 min · confirm live walking route

Take both doubles from the house and see how they handle a real neighbourhood walk. This is deliberately the first leg of the Umeda outing, not a five-minute station hop. Ordinary tiredness is a reason to shorten shopping later; if a child genuinely cannot handle the walk, take the safest fatigue exit.

16:45

Danae's IC Card

Nishi-Kujo Station · operator-assisted Midori-no-Kenbaiki Plus · 08:00–20:00 operator hours

Everyone else is already set. Andrew, Sam, Dan, Connie and Eloise all carry Suicas from February, and Suica and ICOCA are interchangeable nationwide, so those work on every train, subway and bus here without doing anything. William and Savannah are under six and travel free.

That leaves Danae. Having just turned six she now pays child fares, and a child ICOCA applies them automatically. At the Plus machine, call the operator and complete the child-card application. Bring Danae's passport to prove her name and date of birth; a parent can purchase the card for her. Pay ¥2,000 cash, including a refundable ¥500 deposit.

Tip: Operator help is currently 08:00–20:00. If the machine or queue does not work out, buy Danae a child paper ticket for the Loop Line and try the staffed counter at Osaka Station. Recheck station hours near travel.

🚃 Loop Line from Nishi-Kujo to Osaka Station, 3 stops (~6 min). LUCUA 1100 is beside Osaka Station; find Topologie on 2F, then cross to Hanshin for Loft.

~17:40

Loft, Umeda

Hanshin Umeda Main Store, 6F · optional · 10:00–20:00

Umeda is northern Osaka's centre of gravity — a dense knot of department stores, underground malls and railway lines stacked on top of each other. Loft moved in May 2025 — it left Chayamachi and is now on the 6th floor of the Hanshin Umeda Main Store, which puts it directly above the station rather than a walk away. Older guides still send you to Chayamachi. If there is energy after Topologie, browse stationery, kitchen gadgets, stickers, pens and small clever objects you didn't know existed.

It's also a gentle place to be jet-lagged. Bright, warm, no decisions required; leave the shop whenever you've had enough and use another easy Umeda stop until 20:00.

Tip: The stationery floor is the one people lose an hour on. Consider yourself warned.

~18:10

grenier

Hankyu Grand Building 1F · optional · 10:00–20:00 · Connie's request

A tiny French pastry shop on the ground floor of the Hankyu Grand Building, and the reason this stop is on the itinerary at all. Their mille-feuille is the thing to get — layers of very thin, very crisp pastry with custard cream, and a strawberry version that's the signature. They sell out of things, so it pays not to be last through the door.

It's takeaway only: no seating inside, just a small bench outside. So this is a grab-and-carry stop rather than a sit-down one, which is why it lands before dinner rather than after.

Tip: Closing time is 20:00 and their days off are irregular rather than fixed — worth a glance at Maps before we set out.

Easy dinner — kaiten sushi if it fits

Umeda · choose on the night · no reservation

Conveyor-belt sushi would be a fun first dinner if everyone has energy for it. Pick a convenient place and check the queue on the night. If that feels like too much, choose simpler food in Umeda and follow it with dessert or a quiet indoor wander; there is no restaurant booking to protect.

Tip: Keep the meal easy. The stroller walk and child card are already a full arrival-night achievement; the remaining goal is simply to stay out until at least 20:00.

20:00

Stay out through 20:00

Earliest ordinary departure from Umeda · not bedtime

Let the evening reach at least 20:00 Japan time before starting the trip home. Browsing is not required: an easy meal, dessert or sheltered wander counts. If a child is genuinely overwhelmed, leave sooner and use the safest return.

🚃 After 20:00, take the Loop Line to Nishi-Kujo, then allow another 25–35 minutes walking home with the strollers. If that last leg is too much, use a cargo-capable taxi.
~20:50

Home, and Sleep

Konohana · Day one done

That's enough for one day. You've crossed the Pacific and the date line, cleared customs, tested the strollers, learned a train line and eaten your first dinner in Japan — and you're going to bed at a genuinely reasonable local hour.

The mille-feuille, if it made it this far, is best eaten tonight rather than saved.

Tip: Expect to wake at 4am anyway. It passes in a couple of days — and tomorrow's Osaka walking tour is the cure.

If you'd rather bail: genuinely, it's fine. If you get to the house and the idea of going back out is unbearable, stay in — we'll bring you a pastry. There are five and a half more weeks of this and nobody is keeping score on day one.
