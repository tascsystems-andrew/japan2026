Arrival Day

Straight Off the Plane and Into Umeda

You've been on a plane overnight and your body thinks it's the middle of yesterday — as far as your internal clock is concerned it's still the evening of November 5. The plan is deliberately easy: land late morning, an unhurried afternoon at the house, then out for a few hours in Umeda. Nothing here is compulsory. If you'd rather sleep, sleep.

But the standing advice for eastbound jet lag is to stay up until a local bedtime, and an evening walking around a bright, busy district is a far easier way to do that than sitting in a quiet room fighting your eyelids.

The one thing with a clock on it is the airport. KE723 lands at 10:45 and the driver tracks the flight, waiting free for 90 minutes from touchdown — so we want to be out of the arrivals hall by about 12:15. That is comfortable for eight people if we keep moving, and it is the reason we don't stop for anything at the airport. Everything after the car is ours to shuffle.
You'll want a Suica or ICOCA card — the tap-to-ride card that works on every train, subway and bus for the whole trip, and in most vending machines and convenience stores. We're going by car from the airport so you won't need one to get home, and with the driver on a 90-minute clock we won't stop for them at KIX. We'll pick them up at Nishikujo on the way into Umeda instead, which takes two minutes at the machines.
10:45

Land at Kansai Airport

KIX · Immigration, bags, customs

KIX is built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, so the approach is all water right up until the wheels touch. Immigration and customs usually run about an hour — Japan's entry process is orderly and unhurried, and it goes faster if you fill in the arrival card on the plane rather than at the desk.

Our driver is already tracking the flight and will wait free for 90 minutes from landing, so the target is to be through and outside by about 12:15. With eight of us and a pile of bags that means heading for the exit rather than browsing. If we do run over, the surcharge is ¥2,000 per half hour paid to the driver in cash — not a disaster, just worth avoiding.

Meeting point is Terminal 1, 1F — exit C if we come out of the north exit, exit D if it's the south. The driver waits outside the doors with a name board.

Tip: There are free water fountains past customs, and the airport toilets are worth experiencing on their own terms. Consider it your first cultural encounter.

🚗 Straight out to the car — the driver meets us at the 1F exit with a name board.
13:15

The House in Konohana

Kasugadekita · A short walk from Nishikujo Station

The drive out crosses the Sky Gate Bridge — the span that ties the airport island to the mainland — then runs up along the bay before the city closes in around you. An undemanding first look at Osaka through a car window.

Our Airbnb for the six weeks sits in a quiet residential pocket of Konohana Ward. It's a real neighbourhood rather than a tourist strip — vending machines on the corners, bicycles everywhere, a convenience store at the end of the road. Nishikujo Station is a few minutes' walk away and puts most of Kansai within an hour.

You've got a couple of unhurried hours here — unpack properly, shower, work out which switch does what. The one thing to resist is the bed. We'll head back out around 16:30.

Tip: A shower and a change of clothes does more for jet lag than a nap does. The nap is a trap — at this hour it's the difference between sleeping tonight and being wide awake at 3am.

🚃 JR Osaka Loop Line from Nishikujo → Osaka Station, 2 stops (~5 min). Umeda is the station district itself.
16:45

Loft, Umeda

Umeda · Free to browse · Typically until 21:00

Umeda is northern Osaka's centre of gravity — a dense knot of department stores, underground malls and railway lines stacked on top of each other. Loft is the best possible first stop: several floors of stationery, kitchen gadgets, skincare, stickers, pens and small clever objects you didn't know existed. It's the shop that explains why people come back from Japan with a suitcase of things they can't quite justify.

It's also a gentle place to be jet-lagged. Bright, warm, no decisions required, and you can leave whenever you've had enough.

Tip: The stationery floor is the one people lose an hour on. Consider yourself warned.

grenier

Hankyu Grand Building 1F · 10:00–20:00 · Connie's request

A tiny French pastry shop on the ground floor of the Hankyu Grand Building, and the reason this stop is on the itinerary at all. Their mille-feuille is the thing to get — layers of very thin, very crisp pastry with custard cream, and a strawberry version that's the signature. They sell out of things, so it pays not to be last through the door.

It's takeaway only: no seating inside, just a small bench outside. So this is a grab-and-carry stop rather than a sit-down one, which is why it lands before dinner rather than after.

Tip: Closing time is 20:00 and their days off are irregular rather than fixed — worth a glance at Maps before we set out.

18:45

Sushi Train Welcome Dinner

Umeda · Your first meal in Japan

Conveyor-belt sushi is the right first dinner: no menu to decipher, no ordering to negotiate, and you eat as much or as little as jet lag allows. Plates come past on the belt, you take what you like, and the colour of the plate tells you the price. Most places also have a touchscreen for ordering something made fresh, usually with English.

Green tea is free — there's powdered matcha at the table and a hot water tap. Stack your plates as you go; they count them at the end.

Tip: Don't over-order at the start. It's easy to build a tower of plates before you notice you're full and it's 3am in your head.

🚃 Osaka Station → Nishikujo on the Loop Line, 2 stops (~5 min), then the short walk home.
20:30

Home, and Sleep

Konohana · Day one done

That's enough for one day. You've crossed the Pacific and the date line, cleared customs, learned a train line and eaten your first sushi in Japan — and you're going to bed at a genuinely reasonable local hour, which is the single best thing you can do for tomorrow.

The mille-feuille, if it made it this far, is best eaten tonight rather than saved.

Tip: Expect to wake at 4am anyway. It passes in a couple of days — and tomorrow's Osaka walking tour is the cure.

If you'd rather bail: genuinely, it's fine. If you get to the house and the idea of going back out is unbearable, stay in — we'll bring you a pastry. There are five and a half more weeks of this and nobody is keeping score on day one.
