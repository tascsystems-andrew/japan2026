Girls' Kyoto Trip

3 Days · 2 Nights

Kyoto Without the Boys

Andy's back in Vancouver for the week. This is Sam and Adrienne's chance to take the kids to Kyoto — two nights at a great hotel, the iconic sights, and the kind of trip that proves you can navigate a foreign city with four kids under 7 and come out the other side proud of yourself.

The middle day is fully guided. On the Wednesday a private car and an English-speaking guide collect you from the hotel door at 8:00 and bring you back to it — parking, routes and timings all handled, the whole day arranged and paid for in advance. Days 1 and 3 are yours to wander at your own pace; the big day is done for you.
The hotel: Richmond Hotel Premier Kyoto Shijo — right on Shijo-dori with a LIFE supermarket in the basement, free lounge with drinks and snacks all afternoon, and breakfast included.
Day 1 — Tuesday, November 24
Osaka → Kyoto · Nishiki Market · Gion

Morning — Homeschool

Osaka Airbnb

Check In + Drop Bags

Richmond Hotel Premier Kyoto Shijo

Check-in is 2 PM but the hotel will hold luggage. Drop the bags and head straight out — you've got an afternoon to fill.

14:00

Nishiki Market

Nakagyo · Free entry · ~10 min walk from hotel

Kyoto's famous food market — a narrow covered arcade packed with vendors selling pickles, mochi, grilled seafood on sticks, matcha sweets, and everything else Kyoto is known for. This is lunch, snack time, and entertainment all in one. The market is a 10-minute walk from the hotel, making it the perfect first stop.

🧒 Kids love: Choosing snacks from the stalls, the octopus-on-a-stick, the colourful mochi displays, and the general excitement of a market full of food at eye level.

🚶 Walk east through the shopping arcades toward Gion (~15 min)
15:30

Gion

Higashiyama · Free to stroll

Kyoto's famous geisha district. Walk along Hanamikoji-dori — traditional wooden machiya houses, tea houses, and lantern-lit side streets. In the late afternoon you might spot a maiko heading to an evening appointment. The Shirakawa canal area is beautiful with its willow trees and stone bridges.

🧒 Kids love: Spotting people in rental kimonos, the little bridges over the canal, and the vending machine drinks after a lot of walking.

Day 2 — Wednesday, November 25
Full Day Kyoto · Private Car & English-Speaking Guide
You do not have to organise any of today. A private car and an English-speaking guide are booked and paid for, yours for the whole day. They collect you from the hotel door and bring you back to it. No trains, no tickets, no working out which platform, and no walking four tired children to a station at four in the afternoon.

The guide drives, parks, and knows the order to do things in, so you are at the bamboo before the crowds arrive and at the temples before the queues build. Nobody is navigating, nobody is reading a timetable, and nobody is working out where to leave a car.

And it is your car. If everyone is flagging by two o'clock, say so and go back to the hotel. If the kids love somewhere, stay longer. Nothing below is fixed — it is simply the best order to do it in, and the guide will confirm the plan with you the evening before.
7:15

Breakfast at the Hotel

Included · Japanese & Western buffet

The Richmond's breakfast is excellent — Japanese and Western options, good coffee, and enough variety that even picky kids will find something. Start a little earlier than feels reasonable today, so nobody is eating toast standing up when the car pulls in.

8:00

Your Car and Guide Arrive

At the hotel door · Booked and paid

A private air-conditioned vehicle with wifi, an English-speaking guide, and room for all six of you. Walk out of the lobby and get in. That is the entire morning's admin.

Tip: The guide's number will be in your phone before you leave Osaka, and the pickup is confirmed the evening before. You are not chasing anybody.

🚗 Driven to Arashiyama — about 30 minutes, and the guide sorts the parking.
8:30

Arashiyama

Ukyo · Bamboo grove free · First stop, on purpose

The towering bamboo forest, the Togetsukyo Bridge, and the monkey park. Being here at half past eight is the whole point of the early start — the grove is quiet, the light coming through the bamboo is at its best, and by the time the coaches arrive around ten you are walking back to your car.

Walk the grove, cross the bridge, and wander the main street of shops and food stalls. The whole area is flat and stroller-friendly along the main paths.

🧒 Kids love: The bamboo grove (it's magical — the light filters through and the bamboo creaks in the wind), the Togetsukyo Bridge (wide and dramatic), and the Arashiyama Monkey Park if you're up for a 20-min hike to see wild monkeys at the top.

Tip: The Monkey Park (Iwatayama) is a 20-minute uphill climb — fine for the older two, tough with the younger two in a carrier. Tell the guide either way; the car waits.

🚗 Driven across to Kinkaku-ji — about 25 minutes. On the trains this leg is a bus and a transfer.
11:00

Kinkaku-ji — the Golden Pavilion

Kita · About ¥500 at the gate · Open from 9:00

A three-storey pavilion covered in gold leaf, sitting at the edge of a pond that reflects the whole thing straight back at you. It is one of the few places that genuinely looks like the postcard. The visit is a one-way loop around the water — flat, no climbing, thirty to forty minutes at a child's pace.

🧒 Kids love: That it is actually, properly gold. The carp in the pond, the little waterfall, and throwing a coin into the stone bowl for luck near the end of the loop.

Tip: There's a small admission at the gate. The guide handles the queue and the tickets — you walk in.

12:30

Lunch — Wherever the Guide Suggests

Your own · Nothing booked, on purpose

Ask for somewhere with a table, room for a pram and something plain on the menu. The guide will know one nearby and driving to it costs you nothing. This is the part of the day that is far easier with a car than with four children and a train timetable.

🚗 Driven down to Fushimi Inari — about 30 minutes.
14:00

Fushimi Inari Taisha

Fushimi · Free · Open 24 hours

The one everyone knows — thousands of vermilion torii gates winding up a mountain through the forest. You don't need to go anywhere near the summit. The first section up to the Yotsutsuji intersection is about 30 to 45 minutes and has the best gate tunnels and a lookout over Kyoto. That's the sweet spot with kids.

🧒 Kids love: Running through the endless tunnel of orange gates (it feels like a portal to another world), the fox statues everywhere, and the sense of adventure climbing up through the forest. The mini shrines along the path are fun to discover.

Tip: The bottom is busy in the afternoon, but the crowd thins within a few minutes of climbing — most day-trippers photograph the first hundred gates and turn round. Keep walking and it empties out.

And this is the one to drop without a second thought. It's free, it's open around the clock, and if four children have had enough by two o'clock, say the word and the car takes you back to the hotel.

🚗 Driven back to the hotel door — about 20 minutes. The car's job is done.
16:30

Kamo River Walk — If There's Anything Left

Free · Ten minutes from the hotel

Entirely optional, and only if the kids still have legs. The Kamo River is a beautiful spot to let them run — wide gravel banks, shallow water in places, and stepping stones to hop across. Locals jog, picnic and hang about here; it's the city's outdoor living room. In late November the trees along the river may still have their colour.

🧒 Kids love: The stepping stones across the river (turtle and bird shapes!), throwing leaves in the water, and the wide open space to run after a day of temples and cars.
17:00

Evening — Hotel + Dinner

Richmond Hotel Premier Kyoto Shijo

Back to the lounge for round two. For dinner, Pontocho alley (a narrow pedestrian lantern-lit street along the river) is a short walk from the hotel and has restaurants ranging from casual to fancy. Or stay simple — the LIFE supermarket and the hotel room are right there.

Day 3 — Thursday, November 26
Morning Kyoto · Train Home
8:00

Breakfast + Check Out

Richmond Hotel Premier Kyoto Shijo

Last breakfast at the hotel. Check out is 11 AM — pack up, leave luggage at reception if you want a morning stroll, or head straight out.

9:30

Morning Option — Kiyomizu, Nishiki or the Sealed Detour

Your choice

If the group has energy for one more temple, Kiyomizu-dera is the one — the famous wooden terrace jutting out over the hillside with views across all of Kyoto. It's about 20 minutes by bus or taxi from the hotel. The approach street (Matsubara-dori) is lined with souvenir shops and snack stalls.

If a temple feels like too much, a second pass through Nishiki Market is always worth it — pick up omiyage (souvenirs), grab last snacks, and keep it relaxed.

If Sam opened Tuesday's dinner box, finish either option by 10:25 and follow the walking route inside her prize. Leave the Richmond at 10:40 for its 11:00 opening, then return for the bags. The destination stays sealed until she earns it.

12:30

Train Home

JR Kyoto → Osaka (~30 min)

Collect luggage from the hotel, head to Kyoto Station, and catch the train back to Osaka. You'll be home by early afternoon with time for afternoon homeschool and a well-earned rest.

You just did Kyoto with four kids under 7 and no dad. That's not a trip — that's an achievement.
