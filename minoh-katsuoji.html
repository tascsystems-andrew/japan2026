Minoh Falls & the Daruma Temple

Your Last Full Day

The Best Autumn Day in Osaka

This one is entirely optional. You're in Umeda that morning either way — the glasses have to be collected — but what you do with the afternoon is yours, and mooching round the department stores is a perfectly good answer after two and a half weeks on the move. If you've got one more proper day in you, though, this is the one we'd pick.

Minoh is a maple gorge in the hills north of the city, and late November is exactly when it peaks. A paved path follows the river up through the trees to a 33-metre waterfall. Then a short hop over the ridge is Katsuo-ji, a temple where visitors leave daruma dolls after a wish comes true — and after a thousand years of that, they are everywhere. On tree roots, in stone niches, lined up along walls in their hundreds.

The shape of the day — two fixed points. Connie's glasses come first: JINS at Grand Front Osaka opens at 11:00 and she flies out the next morning, so that's the one thing that can't move. And everyone meets at Osaka Station at 18:00 for the farewell teppanyaki. Minoo is about half an hour out from Umeda on the Hankyu, so the sequence is glasses first, then straight out to the gorge for the afternoon. Everything between those two ends is yours.
It's a Sunday on a three-day weekend. November 23 is a national holiday in Japan, so this is peak foliage on the busiest weekend of the season. Because the glasses come first you'll be walking into the gorge at its busiest rather than ahead of it — that's the honest trade. The path is wide and paved, so it's crowded rather than impassable, and the colour is worth it.
Nothing here needs booking. The park is free and open around the clock. Katsuo-ji is ¥500 each, paid at the gate. Your Suica works on every train.
10:15

Leave the House

Nishikujo → Osaka Station → Grand Front

Take the JR Loop Line from Nishikujo to Osaka Station, three stops — Noda, Fukushima, Osaka — about six minutes. Come out on the north side and Grand Front Osaka is straight ahead across the plaza, signposted and mostly under cover.

Tip: You're deliberately not rushing. JINS doesn't open until 11:00, so there's no prize for being there at 10:30.

Collect the Glasses

JINS · Grand Front Osaka, south building 4F · Opens 11:00

Do this first. The progressives were ordered on the 8th, and Connie flies out tomorrow morning — there is no second chance at this. The shop opens at 11:00; allow twenty minutes or so for the fitting and adjustment.

Bring the order slip and her passport. The phone number is 06-6359-1278 if anything looks wrong when you get there.

Tip: Have her wear them out of the shop for ten minutes before you get on the train — progressives take some getting used to, and it's better to find out now than on the flight.

🚶 Back through to Hankyu Osaka-umeda — a separate station from JR Osaka, but signposted the whole way and about five minutes indoors.
11:45

Out to Minoo

Hankyu Osaka-umeda → Minoo

At Hankyu, take the Takarazuka Line to Ishibashi-handai-mae, about 15 minutes. Change there onto the Minoo Line and go three stops to the end of the line, Minoo. About 25–30 minutes from Umeda and roughly ¥280 on your Suica. You'll be there around 12:15.

Tip: The Minoo Line is a short shuttle and Minoo is the last stop, so you cannot overshoot. If in doubt, follow the crowd carrying cameras.

12:30

Lunch in Minoo

Your own · Around the station

Plenty of small places near the station and along the approach to the park — soba, udon, curry, cafés. Nothing booked and nothing fancy; pick whatever looks good and has a queue of locals.

Tip: Eat before the walk, not after. Coming back down at three you'll want a coffee, not a meal.

🚶 Out of Minoo station, turn right and follow the signs — about 10 minutes to the park entrance.
13:15

The Walk Up the Gorge

Minoo Park · Free · Open 24 hours

The first stretch out of Minoo station — a proper paved road with a railing along the river, not a hike. Photographed in early October, before the colour comes in.

From the park entrance it's 2.7 kilometres to the falls, following the river the whole way. Allow 40 to 50 minutes at an unhurried pace. The path is paved for nearly all of it and mostly flat — only the last stretch near the waterfall gets steeper and a bit rocky.

There are tea houses, benches and a few small shrines along the way, so it's the kind of walk you can stop on as often as you like. In late November the whole gorge is red and orange overhead.

Tip: Toilets are at the park entrance and again near the falls, but not much in between.

14:05

Minoh Falls

箕面大滝 · 33 metres

Momiji tempura — real maple leaves, salted, battered and fried, sold in paper bags.

The reward at the top of the valley — a single 33-metre drop into a pool, framed by maples. There's a viewing area and usually a few stalls selling hot drinks.

Try the momiji tempura while you're here — maple leaves, battered and deep-fried, faintly sweet and salty, fried to order at the stalls. It sounds like a novelty and it is, but it's been made in this valley for over a thousand years and it's genuinely good with a coffee.

Tip: Walk back down the same way — it's quicker downhill, about 35 minutes.

🚶 Back down the gorge to Minoo station — about 35 minutes downhill.
15:00

Back at Minoo Station — the Decision

Katsuo-ji only if you're here by 15:30

This is the one rule of the day. If you're standing outside Minoo station by 15:30, there's time for Katsuo-ji. If it's later than that, don't start — have a coffee by the station instead and head back to Umeda with the afternoon still intact.

The maths is unforgiving in both directions: taxi up is 15–20 minutes, last entry at the temple is 16:30, and you need roughly an hour back down and into Osaka Station for dinner at 18:00. Leaving Minoo at 15:30 works. Leaving at 16:00 means either a very hurried temple or a late arrival at your own farewell dinner.

Tip: The gorge on its own is a complete day. Skipping the temple isn't a failure — Katsuo-ji is the bonus, not the point.

🚕 Taxi from Minoo station to Katsuo-ji — about 15–20 minutes, roughly ¥3,000–3,500. See the note below before you set off.
15:50

Katsuo-ji — the Daruma Temple

勝尾寺 · ¥500 each · Normally 8:00–17:00, last entry 16:30

The daruma left behind by people whose wishes came true. They are everywhere.

Founded in the 8th century and known as the temple of winning — katsu means to win, and people have come here for centuries to pray before anything that needs to go their way. When the wish is granted, they bring back a daruma doll and leave it behind.

The result is extraordinary. Thousands upon thousands of small red daruma, tucked into every crevice of the grounds: along walls, on lantern bases, in tree hollows, peering out of the moss. The temple sits in a bowl in the mountains with a pond and a long stone stairway, and in late November the maples around it are the reason half the visitors come.

Tip: Buy a small daruma at the shop, colour in one eye and make a wish. You colour the second eye when it comes true. They travel home easily.

Watch the clock: you have about three quarters of an hour here. Be back in a taxi by 16:45.

🚕 Taxi back down to Minoo station, then the Hankyu in to Osaka-umeda — about an hour door to door, and five minutes on foot through to Osaka Station.
Getting to Katsuo-ji — read this bit. The temple is up a mountain road and the transport is the only fiddly part of the day.

The easy answer is a taxi from outside Minoo station — 15 to 20 minutes, somewhere around ¥3,000–3,500 for the two of you. Show the driver the Japanese below and there's nothing else to arrange.

There is also a Hankyu bus, route 30, which runs 2–3 times an hour from Minoh-Kayano station — a different station from Minoo — taking about 25 minutes for ¥800 each. And on November weekends Hankyu sometimes runs a direct seasonal bus from Minoo itself. If you'd rather bus it, ask at the Minoo station information desk when you arrive at lunchtime and they'll tell you what's running that day — and check the time of the last one back, because the buses stop earlier than you'd think.

Honestly, on your last day, take the taxi.
勝尾寺までお願いします Katsuo-ji made onegai shimasu — "To Katsuo-ji, please." Show this to the taxi driver, or just point at the kanji.
The autumn illumination — not this year, sadly. On November weekends and holidays Katsuo-ji often stays open into the evening with the maples floodlit, and it is genuinely spectacular. Sunset that day is about 16:57, so the good part starts right when you need to be leaving. You can't have both that and the 18:00 teppanyaki, and the dinner is the one everybody's coming to. If the illumination is the thing you'd most like out of the whole day, say so in advance and we'll look at shifting dinner later rather than have you rush it.
What to wear. Comfortable shoes with some grip — the last stretch to the falls is rocky and can be damp. Layers: the gorge is cooler than the city and Katsuo-ji is higher again, so it'll be genuinely cold once the sun drops.
