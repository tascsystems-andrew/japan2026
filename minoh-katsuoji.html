Katsuo-ji, in December

The Best Autumn Day in Osaka

This one is entirely optional. You're in Umeda that morning either way — the glasses have to be collected — but what you do with the afternoon is yours, and mooching round the department stores is a perfectly good answer after two and a half weeks on the move. If you've got one more proper day in you, though, this is the one we'd pick.

Katsuo-ji sits in the hills north of the city and has been the temple of winning for a very long time. People come to ask for a victory, and leave a daruma behind when they get one — so the grounds have accumulated thousands of them, in every crevice and along every path. It is the strangest and best temple on this trip, and it is the one thing here that nobody else will have seen.

Moved, and simplified. This was offered to Dan and Connie for 22 November as a private-car day and they declined, so it is now the family's on Monday 7 December — and it needs no car at all. The Midosuji line was extended to Minoh-Kayano in 2024 and Hankyu bus 30 runs up to the temple in about twenty minutes. Nothing to book. Admission is roughly ¥2,000 for the six of you.

The one hard constraint: the last bus off the mountain is 16:18 on a weekday, and there is no other way down. Train at 09:15, bus from stop 8 at 10:15, temple from 10:40, bus back at 13:15, lunch at the mall by the station, home by about 15:30.
December, not November. The maples will be over — that was the trade in giving up the 22nd. What is left is the temple itself, which is the reason to go: thousands of little red daruma tucked into tree roots, along the streams and up every path. Quieter, colder, and entirely yours.
Nothing here needs booking. The park is free and open around the clock. Katsuo-ji is ¥500 each, paid at the gate. Your Suica works on every train.

Train to Minoh-Kayano

Midosuji line · about 45 minutes

Straight up the Midosuji line to the end of it. The extension to Minoh-Kayano opened in 2024 and is the whole reason this day no longer needs a car — before it, Katsuo-ji meant an infrequent bus from Minoo or a taxi up the mountain.

Tip: this is not Minoo station on the Hankyu, which is where the waterfall is. Different station, different line. The falls are not part of this day.

🚌 Hankyu bus 30 from stop 8, outside Minoh-Kayano. About twenty minutes up.

Katsuo-ji — the daruma temple

勝尾寺 · about ¥2,000 for the six of you · 8:00–17:00

The temple of winning. People have come here for centuries to ask for a victory of one kind or another, and they leave a daruma behind when they get it — so the grounds are covered in thousands of little red figures, tucked into tree roots, lined along the streams, wedged into stone lanterns and window ledges. They accumulate. Nobody tidies them away.

You buy one, pick a goal, and draw a single eye. The second eye goes in when you get there. Then you either leave it somewhere on the grounds or carry it home.

🧒 Kids love: hunting for the smallest daruma hidden in the strangest place. There are thousands and it never stops being funny.

Tip: two and a half hours here is right. It is a hillside, so it is stairs and slopes rather than a flat temple courtyard.

Home

Nishikujo

Back down the Midosuji line. An early finish, on purpose — it is a hill day in December.

Getting to Katsuo-ji — read this bit. The temple is up a mountain road and the transport is the only fiddly part of the day.

The easy answer is a taxi from outside Minoo station — 15 to 20 minutes, somewhere around ¥3,000–3,500 for the two of you. Show the driver the Japanese below and there's nothing else to arrange.

There is also a Hankyu bus, route 30, which runs 2–3 times an hour from Minoh-Kayano station — a different station from Minoo — taking about 25 minutes for ¥800 each. And on November weekends Hankyu sometimes runs a direct seasonal bus from Minoo itself. If you'd rather bus it, ask at the Minoo station information desk when you arrive at lunchtime and they'll tell you what's running that day — and check the time of the last one back, because the buses stop earlier than you'd think.

Honestly, on your last day, take the taxi.
勝尾寺までお願いします Katsuo-ji made onegai shimasu — "To Katsuo-ji, please." Show this to the taxi driver, or just point at the kanji.
The autumn illumination — not this year, sadly. On November weekends and holidays Katsuo-ji often stays open into the evening with the maples floodlit, and it is genuinely spectacular. Sunset that day is about 16:57, so the good part starts right when you need to be leaving. You can't have both that and the 18:00 teppanyaki, and the dinner is the one everybody's coming to. If the illumination is the thing you'd most like out of the whole day, say so in advance and we'll look at shifting dinner later rather than have you rush it.
What to wear. Comfortable shoes with grip — the temple is a hillside of stairs and slopes rather than a flat courtyard, and December mornings up there are cold. Layers, and something warm for standing about while four children hunt for the smallest daruma.
